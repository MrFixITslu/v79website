PART 01 · THE IDEA

A business network is like a small town.

The internet is the road into town. Your firewall is the security gate. Your switch connects different buildings, and Wi-Fi gives mobile devices a way in.

A strong network does two jobs: people can work and people only reach what they are allowed to use. Internet speed alone does not solve every problem.

RISKY SETUP

Guest phones, staff computers and the payment terminal all share one network.

One compromised device could reach places it should not.
SAFER DESIGN

Staff, guests and payment devices are separated, with rules controlling traffic.

Separating networks reduces what one device can access.
Important: This is a planning lesson, not a live network configuration tool. Network rules should be changed by someone with authorised access, a backup and a recovery plan.

Five steps to a better network

  1. Write down every device: staff laptops, printers, payment terminals, cameras and guest devices.
  2. Draw a simple map showing how devices connect to the router or switch.
  3. Protect the router: update its software, change default admin credentials and use WPA2 or WPA3 for Wi-Fi.
  4. Create separate guest access; isolate payment and other sensitive devices based on their requirements.
  5. Test Wi-Fi coverage and connection quality in real rooms, then document changes.

PART 04 · CONNECTIONS

Should you use copper cable or fibre?

For normal office runs, Cat6 or Cat6A copper cabling is common. Four-pair Ethernet channels are generally limited to 100 metres, including patch cords. Fibre is often considered for longer distances, high interference or separate buildings. Outdoor routes require professional assessment.

PART 05 · STORM READINESS

What happens when the power goes off?

In the Caribbean, network planning should consider outages and storm disruptions. A UPS is a battery backup that gives equipment time to keep running briefly or shut down safely. It cannot guarantee internet service when your provider's network is down.

Which items are connected to backup power?

2 of 3 core network components are on your example UPS. Without Wi-Fi power, wireless users lose local access during an outage.

Check the right numbers: Add the power draw of each connected device, allow headroom, then check the UPS's watt rating and manufacturer's runtime chart. A VA number alone cannot tell you how long it will run. Eaton sizing guide ↗

PART 08 · KEEP LEARNING

Useful sources and next steps

Do I need an expensive firewall?

Not necessarily. Start with your real risks, supported equipment, good updates, strong passwords, guest separation, MFA and regular backups. Avoid buying more features than you can maintain.

Does a second Wi-Fi name mean my guests are isolated?

No. Guest isolation depends on the underlying network design and access rules, not just the network name.

Will a UPS keep the internet working during a storm?

It helps your equipment stay powered for a limited time, but the internet provider's connection may still fail. Consider a tested backup connection for critical operations.
