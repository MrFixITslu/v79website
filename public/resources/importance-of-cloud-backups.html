The Critical Importance of Redundant Cloud Backups

A storm, damaged hard drive, ransomware attack or accidental deletion can stop work. A backup plan is useful only when it protects the right records and you can actually restore them.

01 / Get the idea

Start with what matters.

Backups protect copies of data. Disaster recovery brings a working service back. Plan and test both.

02 / Do the work

Five steps you can follow.

Read each step in order. These are practical starting points, not a substitute for a site assessment or specialised advice.

01

Identify critical records

List the records your business needs to serve customers: accounting, bookings, customer records, stock, emails, project files and system configuration. Decide who owns each dataset.

Example: Start with the records that would stop sales or essential operations if lost.

02

Use different copies and locations

The classic 3-2-1 approach means three copies, on two types of storage, with at least one copy offsite. Also consider an offline or immutable copy so ransomware cannot change every copy.

Example: Two folders on one laptop do not protect you from that laptop failing.

03

Automate backups safely

Set an appropriate schedule for each system. Check alerts and backup job results, encrypt sensitive copies and restrict who may delete them.

Example: A successful upload is not the same as a verified backup of every required file.

04

Test recovery with harmless data

Pick a sample folder, restore to a separate safe location and confirm its contents and permissions. Keep the production copy untouched. Record how long the test took.

Example: For complex production systems, use an isolated test environment and a controlled change plan.

05

Agree recovery goals

Recovery Point Objective (RPO) means how much recent data you could lose; Recovery Time Objective (RTO) means how long the service can be unavailable. Discuss what your business can realistically afford.

Example: A 24-hour backup schedule may be unsuitable for a busy system that changes every minute.

03 / Test your judgement

What would you do?

Choose an option. You'll see why it is or isn't a sensible first step.

The situation: Your only copy of customer orders is on an office PC. You also copy files to a USB drive that stays plugged into the same PC.

Create your recovery checklist

Current storage: [DEVICES AND CLOUD SERVICES]

Suggest a 3-2-1 style backup plan, permissions, a safe restore-test schedule and questions for our IT provider. Explain the difference between RPO and RTO. Avoid unsupported recovery guarantees.

My immediate priority: [YOUR MAIN PRIORITY]
Safety first: Use fictional examples when practising. Never paste passwords, payment details, customer records or sensitive information into an unapproved tool. Check facts, costs and technical requirements before making real changes.
