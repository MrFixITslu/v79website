How to Choose an IT Support Provider in Saint Lucia

A support provider should do more than fix computers when they break. Your business needs to know who is responsible, how to report incidents, what happens during an emergency and what is included in the contract.

01 / Get the idea

Start with what matters.

Choose a provider around your needs and evidence, not just the lowest monthly price or a promise to fix everything.

02 / Do the work

Five steps you can follow.

Read each step in order. These are practical starting points, not a substitute for a site assessment or specialised advice.

01

List what your business relies on

Count your devices, locations, internet connections, cloud services and key business applications. Decide which ones are critical during opening hours.

Example: A small shop with a payment terminal needs a different support arrangement from a hotel operating overnight.

02

Check the scope of service

Ask what is covered: remote help, on-site visits, monitoring, updates, backups, network changes, vendor calls and after-hours emergencies. Confirm what is excluded.

Example: A low monthly fee may not include projects, replacement parts or emergency visits.

03

Understand response and resolution

An SLA (Service Level Agreement) describes agreed service measures. Response time is when someone starts handling a ticket; resolution time is when the issue is actually fixed. Ask for both and check the service hours.

Example: A 'one-hour response' does not mean 'fixed in one hour'.

04

Ask about security and continuity

Check MFA, admin account handling, backups, incident procedures, documentation, insurance where relevant, and how the provider would hand over the environment at contract end.

Example: Your organisation should retain ownership of its domains, licences and business data.

05

Compare proposals with a simple scorecard

Use the same questions for each provider. Score business fit, scope, response, evidence, security and total ongoing cost. Request references you can verify.

Example: Avoid unverifiable claims of guaranteed uptime or recovery. Ask what the provider will measure and report.

03 / Test your judgement

What would you do?

Choose an option. You'll see why it is or isn't a sensible first step.

The situation: Two providers submit quotes. Provider A is cheaper but excludes after-hours support. Provider B costs more and clearly states after-hours coverage, support hours, escalation and exclusions.

04 / Make it useful

Build an IT provider comparison

This exercise produces a prompt you can copy into ChatGPT. It doesn't make changes to your business systems.

Help me compare IT support proposals for my Caribbean business.
Business type: [TYPE]
Sites and opening hours: [DETAILS]
Critical systems: [SYSTEMS]
Budget constraints: [BUDGET]

Create a weighted scorecard for scope, response and resolution, security, backups, on-site coverage and total cost. Give me ten questions to ask each provider. Do not invent SLA promises or supplier ratings.

My immediate priority: [YOUR MAIN PRIORITY]
Safety first: Use fictional examples when practising. Never paste passwords, payment details, customer records or sensitive information into an unapproved tool. Check facts, costs and technical requirements before making real changes.
