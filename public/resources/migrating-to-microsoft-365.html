Migrating to Microsoft 365: A Guide for Local Businesses

Microsoft 365 can help your team collaborate, but switching business email is not the same as creating a personal email account. You need to plan the users, licence requirements, DNS records, data transfer and rollback.

02 / Do the work

Five steps you can follow.

Read each step in order. These are practical starting points, not a substitute for a site assessment or specialised advice.

01

Take an inventory before purchasing

List every mailbox, shared address, alias, mailing list, calendar, file share, device and application that sends mail. Verify storage sizes and business needs before selecting licences.

Example: The right Microsoft 365 plan depends on user needs, device management, security and pricing.

02

Prepare accounts and security

Set up the Microsoft 365 tenant, verify your domain, create users and enable suitable MFA. Check administrative recovery access and keep credentials in an approved password manager.

Example: A domain such as yourbusiness.com can remain yours; you are changing where its email is delivered.

03

Run a small pilot first

Choose a test mailbox and migrate a representative sample. Compare emails, folders and calendars where supported. Test sending and receiving from internal and external addresses.

Example: Some migration methods do not include every data type. Identify gaps before the business-wide cutover.

04

Plan DNS and the change window

Make an approved record of existing DNS, then plan MX, Autodiscover, SPF, DKIM and DMARC changes relevant to the selected setup. Coordinate with your domain and DNS administrator.

Example: MX directs incoming email. DNS updates take time to propagate; avoid changing records without a validated plan.

05

Verify and support staff

After cutover, test inbound and outbound mail, mobile devices, Outlook profiles, shared mailboxes, scanners and invoices. Give users a short quick-start guide. Retain rollback information until stable.

Example: Do not cancel the old mail service until the agreed verification and retention requirements are satisfied.

03 / Test your judgement

What would you do?

Choose an option. You'll see why it is or isn't a sensible first step.

The situation: A business has 12 staff email addresses. The owner wants to switch the MX record today, before checking whether the invoicing system sends email.

04 / Make it useful

Build your migration questions

This exercise produces a prompt you can copy into ChatGPT. It doesn't make changes to your business systems.

Help me prepare questions for an IT provider planning a Microsoft 365 migration.
Current mail platform: [CURRENT PLATFORM]
Number of users: [NUMBER]
Shared addresses, apps and devices: [LIST]
Domain/DNS administrator: [PERSON OR PROVIDER]

Create a discovery checklist, a pilot-test plan, a cutover/rollback checklist and a user training list. Flag licence and migration-method dependencies. Do not invent exact costs or claim zero downtime.

My immediate priority: [YOUR MAIN PRIORITY]
Safety first: Use fictional examples when practising. Never paste passwords, payment details, customer records or sensitive information into an unapproved tool. Check facts, costs and technical requirements before making real changes.
