Cybersecurity Best Practices for Saint Lucian SMEs

A cyberattack is not just a computer problem. It can interrupt customer payments, expose private records and stop your team from working. Start with the everyday controls that reduce your biggest risks.

02 / Do the work

Five steps you can follow.

Read each step in order. These are practical starting points, not a substitute for a site assessment or specialised advice.

01

Protect the accounts people use every day

List email, banking, payment, cloud, social media and admin accounts. Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA); use passkeys or other phishing-resistant methods where supported. Remove shared logins and old staff access.

Example: Start with your primary business email account. Anyone who controls it may be able to reset other passwords.

02

Spot phishing before clicking

Check unexpected payment requests, changed bank details, urgent login messages and unusual attachments. Contact the organisation using a number or website you already trust. Do not use the number in the suspicious message.

Example: Practice: a supplier asks you to send money to a 'new' bank account. Call the supplier using a saved number before paying.

03

Separate business and visitor networks

Give customers a guest Wi-Fi network that cannot reach your till, files, printers or administration devices. Change default router credentials and keep Wi-Fi encryption current.

Example: Guest Wi-Fi is convenient, but it should not be a shortcut into business equipment.

04

Update devices and back up data

Turn on supported security updates, remove unused applications, use device protection and maintain recoverable copies of essential business data. Test a restore using harmless sample files.

Example: A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a proven recovery plan.

05

Know what to do when something feels wrong

Make a short response card: who reports incidents, who disables compromised accounts, how payment providers are contacted, and who can approve taking systems offline.

Example: Test the contact list before a real incident. Keep a copy somewhere other than the affected computer.

03 / Test your judgement

What would you do?

Choose an option. You'll see why it is or isn't a sensible first step.

The situation: A member of staff receives an urgent message saying the company payroll account will close unless they click a link and enter their password.

04 / Make it useful

Build a practical security action plan

This exercise produces a prompt you can copy into ChatGPT. It doesn't make changes to your business systems.

Create a simple cybersecurity improvement plan for my small Caribbean business.
System to protect: [SYSTEM]
Team size: [NUMBER]
Current protections: [MFA, BACKUPS, UPDATES, ETC.]

Give me five actions in priority order, including a responsible owner, a safe way to verify completion and a rough effort band. Separate urgent fixes from later improvements. Do not assume we have expensive tools or invent guarantees.

My immediate priority: [YOUR MAIN PRIORITY]
Safety first: Use fictional examples when practising. Never paste passwords, payment details, customer records or sensitive information into an unapproved tool. Check facts, costs and technical requirements before making real changes.
